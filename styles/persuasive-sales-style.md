# Persuasive Sales Style

## What This Style Is

Compelling writing designed to persuade action. Focuses on customer problem, solution, and value proposition. Uses benefit-driven language and social proof.

**Core principle**: Problem → Solution → Proof → Action.

## When to Use It

- Sales emails and pitches
- Outreach to prospects
- Proposal cover letters
- Compelling value propositions
- Investor pitches
- Marketing copy

## When NOT to Use It

- Internal communication (use Consulting)
- Technical specifications (use Technical Documentation)
- Formal governance (use Board Paper)
- Quick decisions (use BLUF)

## Core Structure

```
[HOOK]
Open with the problem or insight they recognize.

[PROBLEM]
Amplify why the problem matters.

[SOLUTION]
How you solve it (your offer).

[PROOF]
Why you're credible (case study, stats, customer testimony).

[VALUE]
What they gain (financial, operational, strategic).

[CALL TO ACTION]
Specific next step with clear ask.
```

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a sales copywriter. Write a persuasive sales email from:

TARGET PROSPECT:
[Who you're selling to and their role]

THEIR PROBLEM:
[What pain point do they experience]

YOUR SOLUTION:
[How you solve it]

KEY DIFFERENTIATOR:
[Why you vs. alternatives]

PROOF:
[Customer success story, data, or metrics]

THE ASK:
[What specific action do you want]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Open with insight they recognize
2. Build urgency around problem
3. Position solution as obvious choice
4. Provide proof (stats, case study, or reference)
5. Emphasise their gain, not your product
6. End with specific, low-friction ask
7. Keep tone conversational but professional

Output only the sales email. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Subject: Cut Your Data Pipeline Build Time by 70% – DataMesh Case Study**

Hi Jordan,

Most data teams spend 3 months building custom pipelines from scratch. Testing. Debugging. Refactoring. You're probably spending the same.

We work with companies like yours—mid-sized teams with limited data engineering staff. They were frustrated by the months it took to go from raw data to analytics-ready pipelines.

That's why we built DataMesh. It eliminates the boilerplate. No custom code. Just YAML configuration. Most teams cut their pipeline build time from 3 months to 3 weeks.

One financial services firm we work with went from 6-month analytics cycle to 2 weeks. Their CFO now gets insights in real time instead of quarterly. That's a huge competitive advantage.

If cutting 70% off your data pipeline timeline interests you, I'd love to show you a quick 20-minute demo. We can walk through a pipeline similar to what you're building and show exactly where you'd save time.

Are you free Tuesday or Wednesday for a brief call?

Jordan

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Hook addresses a real problem
- [ ] Problem is compelling and timely
- [ ] Solution is clear and specific
- [ ] Proof (case study or metric) is credible
- [ ] Value is customer-focused, not product-focused
- [ ] Call to action is specific and low-friction
- [ ] Tone is conversational and authentic
- [ ] Email is under 200 words

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Features-focused not benefits | Reader doesn't care about your product | Focus on what they gain |
| Weak social proof | Claim lacks credibility | Use specific metrics or customer reference |
| Vague call to action | Prospect doesn't know what to do | Ask for specific next step: "15-min call Tuesday?" |
| Too much context | Prospect loses interest | Keep email short; details for follow-up |
| Salesy tone | Prospect disengages | Write conversationally; be authentic |
