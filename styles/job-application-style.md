# Job Application Style

## What This Style Is

Compelling personal writing for job applications. Shows why you're qualified and interested. Authentic and targeted, not generic.

**Core principle**: Why You → Why Them → What You'd Bring → Next Steps.

## When to Use It

- Cover letters for specific roles
- Job application essays
- LinkedIn "About" section
- Networking outreach
- Career change explanations
- Visa sponsorship statements

## When NOT to Use It

- Formal business communication (use Board Paper)
- Executive updates (use Smart Brevity)
- Technical specifications (use Technical Documentation)
- Teaching or training (use Socratic)

## Core Structure

```
[OPENING]
Why this role, why this company.

[RELEVANT EXPERIENCE]
Specific examples of relevant skills.

[WHAT EXCITES YOU]
What you bring; why you care.

[CALL TO ACTION]
Next step; contact details.
```

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a career coach. Write a cover letter from:

ROLE:
[Job title and company]

YOUR BACKGROUND:
[Relevant experience]

WHAT ATTRACTED YOU:
[Why this role appeals to you]

WHAT YOU'D BRING:
[How your skills fit their needs]

CAREER GOAL:
[Where you want to go; how this fits]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Write conversationally but professionally
2. Show you've researched the company
3. Use specific examples from your background
4. Connect your experience to their needs
5. Show genuine interest
6. Keep to 1 page

Output only the cover letter. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Cover Letter – Senior Product Manager, DataMesh Analytics**

I'm writing about the Senior Product Manager role at DataMesh. Your platform solves a problem I've watched teams struggle with for years—getting value from data without massive engineering investment.

In my current role at FinTech Solutions, I led product for our data integration suite. We grew from 0 to 50 customers in 18 months, largely because we obsessed over ease-of-use. Instead of requiring data engineers, we built APIs that product managers could integrate in hours. That experience taught me how to balance power with simplicity—exactly what DataMesh seems to be doing.

What excites me about this role:

**The Product**: Your design decisions are thoughtful. The query builder is elegant; the monitoring suite is genuinely useful. I'd love to help shape what comes next.

**The Team**: Your team has deep domain expertise. I'd learn from people who've built data systems at scale, and I'd bring product thinking to technical complexity.

**The Market**: Open-source adoption into enterprise is fascinating. You're at the intersection of developer adoption and enterprise sales—a dynamic I want to explore.

I'm confident I can help you:
- Expand into enterprise (I've done this twice)
- Make hard trade-offs between feature parity and simplicity
- Build a product roadmap investors care about

I'd love to discuss how my experience fits your needs. I'm available for a call any time this week.

Best regards,
Jordan

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Opening explains why this specific role interests you
- [ ] You've researched the company (shows in details)
- [ ] Examples are specific, not generic
- [ ] You address their needs, not just your wants
- [ ] Tone is authentic and conversational
- [ ] Length is one page
- [ ] Call to action is clear and specific

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Generic opening | Feels like a template | Show you've researched the specific company |
| Too much about you, not them | Sounds self-focused | Connect your skills to their needs |
| Vague achievements | Can't assess your qualifications | Use specific examples with metrics |
| Too long | Gets skimmed or ignored | Keep to one page maximum |
| No genuine interest shown | Sounds like you'll take any job | Show you've thought about why this role |
