# Academic Style

## What This Style Is

Formal, evidence-based writing for academic and research contexts. Emphasises argument, evidence, and scholarly credibility. Uses citations and structured reasoning.

**Core principle**: Thesis → Argument → Evidence → Conclusion.

## When to Use It

- Research papers and theses
- Academic journal submissions
- Scholarly analysis
- Literature reviews
- Policy papers with academic rigour
- Educational content at advanced level

## When NOT to Use It

- Business communication (use Consulting Style)
- Quick updates (use Smart Brevity)
- General audience (use Plain English)
- Teaching beginners (use Socratic)

## Core Structure

```
[TITLE AND ABSTRACT]
Summary of argument and findings.

[INTRODUCTION]
Context; thesis statement; scope.

[LITERATURE REVIEW]
What prior research says; gaps you address.

[METHODOLOGY]
How you conducted research or analysis.

[FINDINGS OR ARGUMENT]
Core analysis; evidence; reasoning.

[DISCUSSION]
Implications; limitations; future research.

[CONCLUSION]
Restatement of thesis; contribution.

[REFERENCES]
Cited sources in standard format.
```

## Short Example

**Engineering Practices and Team Productivity – Introduction**

Team productivity in software engineering is influenced by multiple factors: tooling, process, and knowledge management. Prior research (DeMarco & Lister, 1987) identified environmental factors as primary productivity drivers. However, recent scaling challenges suggest that knowledge sharing and onboarding practices are increasingly critical as teams grow.

This paper investigates how architectural documentation and mentorship structures correlate with junior engineer productivity in growing teams. We hypothesise that structured onboarding and accessible architectural knowledge reduce time-to-productivity for new engineers by 30–50%, measured across deployment capability and feature velocity.

[Thesis statement; methodology would follow...]

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Thesis is clear and debatable
- [ ] Evidence supports argument
- [ ] Relevant sources are cited
- [ ] Limitations are acknowledged
- [ ] Structure follows logical progression
- [ ] Formal tone throughout
- [ ] References are complete and consistent

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Weak thesis | Argument is unclear | State thesis explicitly; make it debatable |
| Unsupported claims | Lacks credibility | Back with evidence or citations |
| Missing citations | Appears plagiarised | Cite all borrowed ideas |
| Vague methodology | Results not reproducible | Explain exactly what you did |
| No limitations | Overstates findings | Acknowledge scope and constraints |
