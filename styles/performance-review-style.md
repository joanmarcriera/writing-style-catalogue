# Performance Review Style

## What This Style Is

Constructive, specific feedback on employee performance. Balanced assessment of strengths and development areas. Focused on growth and clarity.

**Core principle**: Specific → Constructive → Growth-Focused.

## When to Use It

- Annual or periodic performance reviews
- 360-degree feedback
- Promotion assessments
- Feedback to employees
- Talent development conversations
- Performance improvement plans

## When NOT to Use It

- Disciplinary action (legal counsel required)
- Quick feedback (use in-person or brief note)
- Public announcements (use Crisis Communications)
- Formal governance (use Board Paper)

## Core Structure

```
[OVERVIEW]
Overall assessment; context of review period.

[STRENGTHS]
Specific, observable strengths with examples.

[DEVELOPMENT AREAS]
Specific areas for growth; non-judgmental framing.

[ACHIEVEMENTS THIS PERIOD]
Key accomplishments and impact.

[DEVELOPMENT PLAN]
Specific growth goals; support provided.

[OVERALL RATING]
Clear performance rating if applicable.

[NEXT STEPS]
Follow-up meeting; goals for next period.
```

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are an HR professional. Write a performance review from:

EMPLOYEE:
[Name and role]

REVIEW PERIOD:
[Dates covered]

CONTEXT:
[Significant projects or changes]

STRENGTHS OBSERVED:
[Specific examples of strong performance]

DEVELOPMENT AREAS:
[Areas where growth would help]

KEY ACCOMPLISHMENTS:
[Major achievements this period]

GROWTH GOALS:
[What should they work on next]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Be specific with examples
2. Balance strengths and development areas (roughly 60/40)
3. Use constructive, non-judgmental language
4. Tie feedback to business impact
5. Provide clear development plan
6. Leave space for employee response

Output only the review. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Performance Review: Alex Chen – Senior Engineer**

**Review Period**: 1 January – 31 December 2025

**Overall Assessment**
Alex has been a strong performer this year, delivering significant technical contributions and beginning to mentor junior engineers. There's clear growth in both technical depth and leadership.

**Strengths**

*Technical Excellence*
Alex led the migration of our payment processing system to Kubernetes, delivering on time and under budget. Architectural decisions were thoughtful; technical documentation was exemplary. This project had strategic importance; the execution was excellent.

*Mentorship*
Alex has taken on mentoring two junior engineers. Their feedback is detailed, supportive, and specific. Mentees have grown visibly this year; this is directly attributable to Alex's time investment.

*Collaboration*
Works well cross-functionally. Communicates clearly in meetings and written updates. Other teams actively seek Alex's input on technical decisions.

**Development Areas**

*Project Management*
While technical delivery is strong, project planning could improve. The payment system project ran short on contingency twice because timeline estimates didn't account for integration testing. Alex is thoughtful about this; would benefit from training in estimation and planning.

*Public Speaking*
Alex has declined opportunities to present at conferences and internal tech talks. Public visibility would help career progression. Encouraging experimentation here.

**Achievements This Year**
- Led Kubernetes migration project (2 teams, 4 months, on time)
- Mentored 2 junior engineers; both grew visibly
- Authored 3 technical design documents used as team references
- Reduced API response time by 30% through optimization work

**Development Goals for 2026**
- Take a public speaking training (internal or external)
- Lead one conference talk or workshop
- Develop junior engineer into independent architect
- Take on one strategic technical initiative (mentoring + leading)

**Rating**: Exceeds Expectations

**Next Steps**
We'll discuss these points in a follow-up meeting. Alex, please share your perspective on the review and your goals for 2026.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Specific examples back up all feedback
- [ ] Balance of strengths and development areas (roughly 60/40)
- [ ] Language is constructive, not judgmental
- [ ] Development plan is concrete and supportive
- [ ] Tied to business impact where relevant
- [ ] Encourages growth and dialogue
- [ ] Clear on expectations going forward

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Vague feedback | Employee doesn't know how to improve | Use specific examples, not generalities |
| All positive or all critical | Lacks credibility; demoralising | Balance strengths and growth areas |
| Surprise feedback | Undermines trust; should be ongoing | Review should contain no surprises |
| No development plan | Employee doesn't know what to do next | Provide specific, supported goals |
| Too long or unfocused | Employee overwhelmed | Keep to 2 pages; stay focused |
