# Plain English

## What This Style Is

Plain English is jargon-free, active, and direct. Short sentences. Simple words. Clear structure. Designed for any audience, regardless of expertise.

**Core principle**: Clarity over cleverness. Simplest word that works, always.

## When to Use It

- Instructions and how-to guides
- Explanations for mixed-audience readers
- Customer communications
- Change announcements
- Policy summaries
- Accessibility for non-specialists
- Any document where clarity is paramount

## When NOT to Use It

- Formal legal documents (use Legal Professional)
- Technical precision required (use Technical Documentation)
- Persuasive recommendations (use Minto or Consulting)
- Complex arguments (use Board Paper)
- Academic writing (use Academic Style)

## Core Principles

**Use simple words**
- "Use" not "utilise"
- "Start" not "commence"
- "Stop" not "cease"
- "Help" not "facilitate"

**Use active voice**
- "We improved performance" not "Performance was improved"
- "The team decided" not "A decision was made"

**Use short sentences**
- Average 15–20 words per sentence
- One idea per sentence
- Break complex thoughts into multiple sentences

**Use clear structure**
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max)
- Headings to break content
- Bullet points for lists
- White space for readability

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a Plain English expert. Convert the following text into Plain English:

TEXT:
[Paste your text here]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Replace jargon with simple words
2. Use active voice throughout
3. Keep sentences under 20 words
4. Break paragraphs at 3–4 sentences
5. Add headers and bullet points
6. Use "you" and "we" where appropriate
7. Remove unnecessary words

Check your output against:
- Any word a 12-year-old wouldn't know?
- Any sentence over 20 words?
- Any passive voice?
- Any jargon or abbreviations without explanation?

Output only the Plain English version. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Before (Jargon-heavy):**
"The implementation of the aforementioned utilisation protocols necessitates comprehensive stakeholder engagement and iterative refinement mechanisms."

**After (Plain English):**
"We need to work with the team and test the plan before we put it in place."

## Plain English Rules

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Use simple words | "Use" not "utilise"; "start" not "commence" |
| Active voice | "We cut costs" not "Costs were cut" |
| Short sentences | "15–20 words maximum" |
| One idea per sentence | Split complex thoughts |
| Address the reader | "You can" not "One can"; "We are" not "It is" |
| Concrete language | "Three examples" not "various instances" |
| Avoid jargon | Explain or remove technical terms |
| No double negatives | "Easy" not "not difficult" |
| Numbers as digits | "5 days" not "five days" |
| White space | Short paragraphs; headers; lists |

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] No words over 3 syllables (or explained if necessary)
- [ ] All sentences under 20 words
- [ ] Active voice throughout
- [ ] No jargon or initialisms without explanation
- [ ] Paragraphs are 3–4 sentences maximum
- [ ] Headings break content clearly
- [ ] Lists use bullet points, not paragraphs
- [ ] Accessible to 12-year-old readers

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Jargon without explanation | Reader doesn't understand | Use simple words or define jargon |
| Long sentences | Hard to parse | Break into shorter sentences |
| Passive voice | Unclear who did what | Use active: "We decided" not "It was decided" |
| Complex structure | Reader gets lost | Use headers and short paragraphs |
| Unnecessary words | Wastes reader time | Cut adverbs and qualifiers |
| Acronyms unexplained | Excludes readers | Spell out on first use or avoid |
| Dense paragraphs | Looks hard to read | Keep to 3–4 sentences max |
