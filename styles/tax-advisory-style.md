# Tax Advisory Style

## What this style is

Tax advisory style is the professional register used by qualified tax advisers to communicate technical analysis to clients, colleagues, or governance bodies. It follows a strict logical structure: the question being answered is stated first, the relevant facts are set out clearly, the applicable law or guidance is applied to those facts, and a clear conclusion — with a stated confidence level — is reached. Where uncertainty remains, it is named and its implications are described.

This style exists because tax questions are legally consequential. The quality of the documentation matters in its own right: a well-structured technical note creates a defensible record of the adviser's reasoning, supports the client's compliance position, and reduces the risk that an ambiguous conclusion is acted upon incorrectly. The adviser's role is not simply to state a conclusion but to show the reasoning that supports it.

**Core principle**: Question clearly stated, analysis thorough and fact-anchored, conclusion explicit with a confidence level, uncertainties named.

## When to use it

- Tax technical notes for clients on specific questions of law or application
- Adviser-to-client memos documenting a recommended tax position
- Board or audit committee tax papers summarising material tax risks
- Transaction tax analysis for M&A, restructuring, or financing transactions
- Compliance technical notes on whether a specific treatment is defensible
- Transfer pricing documentation and policy notes
- Employer tax advice on employment status, benefits, or share scheme questions

## When not to use it

- General business communication where tax is one of many topics — use [Consulting Style](consulting-style.md) instead
- Marketing materials for tax advisory services — use [Persuasive Sales Style](persuasive-sales-style.md) instead
- Operational guidance for non-specialist staff — use [Plain English](plain-english.md) instead
- Teaching or onboarding to tax concepts — use [Socratic Explainer](socratic-explainer.md) instead

## Core structure

```
[TITLE AND DATE]
Tax technical note title, client name or matter reference, date.
Prepared by and reviewed by (where applicable).
Status: Draft or Final.

[QUESTION]
The specific tax question being analysed.
One or two sentences — precise and complete.

[EXECUTIVE SUMMARY] (optional for longer notes)
2–3 sentences: the conclusion and confidence level.
Key caveats or conditions.

[FACTS AND ASSUMPTIONS]
Relevant facts as provided by the client or instructing party.
Explicitly stated assumptions where facts are not confirmed.
Material limitations on the scope of the note.

[ANALYSIS]
Application of the relevant law, guidance, or case law to the facts.
Organised by issue if multiple points arise.
Reference to statutory provisions, HMRC guidance, and case law.
Examination of alternative interpretations or challenges.

[CONCLUSION]
A clear, direct answer to the question.
Confidence level: High / Medium / Low with brief explanation.
Any conditions on which the conclusion depends.

[NEXT STEPS AND DOCUMENTATION]
What the client should do as a result of this analysis.
Documentation to be prepared or retained.
Filing or notification obligations (if any).
Whether further enquiry or HMRC clearance is advisable.

[APPENDICES]
Statutory references.
Detailed calculations.
Supporting HMRC guidance extracts.
```

## Copy-paste prompt

```
You are a qualified tax adviser writing a formal tax technical note. Your task is to analyse the following tax question and produce a professional tax advisory document.

AUDIENCE: [AUDIENCE — e.g. "Group Finance Director of a UK-resident company", "Board Audit Committee", "In-house legal counsel reviewing a transaction structure"]
PURPOSE: [PURPOSE — e.g. "Advise on the UK tax treatment of carried interest received by an employee", "Document the VAT position on cross-border digital services supplied to non-UK businesses", "Analyse whether a proposed group restructure triggers a degrouping charge"]
CONTEXT: [CONTEXT — e.g. "Transaction is expected to complete in Q3 2026; client needs a documented position before signing", "Three prior advisers have given conflicting views; client needs a definitive note for the audit committee"]
INPUT: [INPUT — paste all relevant facts, the client's description of the transaction or situation, any statutory references or guidance you wish to include, and your initial analysis here]
DESIRED_OUTCOME: [DESIRED_OUTCOME — e.g. "Client has a documented tax technical position they can rely on for compliance purposes and share with their auditors"]
TONE: [TONE — e.g. "Professional and precise", "Authoritative but accessible to a non-specialist finance director", "Technical — written for an in-house tax team"]
LENGTH: [LENGTH — e.g. "800–1,200 words" or "Full technical note of up to 2,500 words"]
FORMAT: [FORMAT — headed sections: Question, Facts and Assumptions, Analysis, Conclusion, Next Steps]
CONSTRAINTS: [CONSTRAINTS — e.g. "This note must not constitute advice on non-UK jurisdictions", "Do not include any client-identifying information in the body of the note", "Statutory references must be to the current consolidated version of the legislation"]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. State the tax question precisely at the top of the note. The question must be answerable — not a topic description but a specific legal question.
2. Set out the relevant facts in a clearly headed section. Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions. Label each assumption explicitly as "(assumed)" or "on the basis that…"
3. Identify the applicable statutory provisions, HMRC guidance, and relevant case law. Reference specific section numbers (e.g. ITA 2007 s.64, TCGA 1992 s.30).
4. Apply the law to the facts logically, step by step. Address alternative interpretations or risks where they exist.
5. State a conclusion that directly answers the question. Include a confidence level (High / Medium / Low) with a brief explanation of what drives the confidence level.
6. Identify any conditions on which the conclusion depends. Flag if facts change, the conclusion may change.
7. Include a Next Steps section covering: documentation to retain, any filing obligations, whether HMRC clearance would be advisable, and any related actions.
8. Use British English throughout (colour, organisation, licence, favour, analyse, practise, etc.).
9. Preserve all facts exactly as provided in [INPUT]. Do not introduce new facts or change figures. Where the analysis requires a fact that is not provided, state the assumption clearly.
10. This is a template document. Include a disclaimer that it should be reviewed by a qualified tax adviser before being relied upon.

Output the tax technical note only. No preamble, no commentary.
```

## Output format

A tax technical note is a formal professional document, typically delivered as a PDF or Word document on headed paper. It contains:

- **Title block**: Note title, client or matter reference, date, author, reviewer, and status
- **Headed sections**: Question, Facts and Assumptions, Analysis, Conclusion, Next Steps
- **Statutory references**: Cited in the text — e.g. "under ITA 2007 s.83(1)(a)" — and listed in an appendix
- **Confidence level**: Stated explicitly in the Conclusion section (High / Medium / Low)
- **Caveat and disclaimer**: Standard professional disclaimer at the end
- **No bullet points in the Analysis section**: The Analysis uses structured prose with logical connectives to show reasoning — not a list of points

Length varies significantly: a short technical note on a defined question is 500–800 words; a multi-issue transaction analysis may run to 3,000 words. Client-facing notes should be written at a level appropriate for the stated audience — a note for a Finance Director requires clear English with key terms explained; a note for an in-house tax team can use full technical register.

## Example

**Tax Technical Note**
*Matter: Employment-related Securities — Restricted Stock Units*
*Client: [Confidential] | Prepared by: Tax Advisory Team | Date: 14 May 2026 | Status: Final*

---

**Question**

Whether restricted stock units (RSUs) awarded under the Company's Long-Term Incentive Plan (LTIP) to UK-resident employees vest and are taxable under the employment-related securities rules of ITEPA 2003, and, if so, at what point the charge arises and how it is calculated.

**Facts and Assumptions**

The following facts have been provided by the client:

1. The Company is incorporated in Delaware (USA) and listed on NASDAQ. It has a UK subsidiary ("UK Sub") that employs approximately 340 staff in the United Kingdom.
2. UK Sub employees are awarded RSUs under the LTIP. Each RSU represents the right to receive one share in the parent company on vesting.
3. RSUs vest over a four-year period: 25% after year one, then monthly over the following 36 months.
4. On vesting, shares are transferred directly to the employee's brokerage account. No payment is required from the employee.
5. The LTIP does not include a performance condition beyond continued employment.

The following assumptions are made, on the basis of information provided:

- The RSUs do not constitute enterprise management incentives (EMI) or any HMRC-approved scheme (assumed — no scheme registration has been mentioned).
- The parent company shares are readily convertible assets (RCAs) as defined by ITEPA 2003 s.702, given the NASDAQ listing (assumed — to be confirmed with client).

**Analysis**

*Statutory framework*

RSUs awarded to UK-resident employees in respect of their employment fall within the employment-related securities (ERS) legislation in ITEPA 2003, Part 7. Where securities are acquired by reason of employment, the charge arises under Chapter 2 (restricted securities) or, if there is no restriction, Chapter 1 (unrestricted securities).

In this case, the RSUs are subject to a forfeiture condition on cessation of employment during the vesting period. This constitutes a "restriction" within the meaning of ITEPA 2003 s.423 — specifically, a restriction affecting the freedom to retain the securities. Accordingly, the RSUs are restricted securities until each tranche vests.

*Timing of charge*

Under ITEPA 2003 s.426(1), a chargeable event arises when a restriction is lifted. For RSUs vesting monthly over four years, a chargeable event arises on each vesting date as employment restrictions are lifted on the shares transferred.

*Amount of charge*

Under ITEPA 2003 s.428, the charge is calculated as the "unrestricted market value" (UMV) of the shares at the point of vesting, less any amount paid by the employee. Here, no amount is paid by the employee, so the full UMV of the shares vested in each tranche is chargeable to income tax under ITEPA 2003 s.7.

*PAYE and NICs*

Because the parent company shares are (assumed to be) readily convertible assets under ITEPA 2003 s.702, the employer (UK Sub) is required to operate PAYE on the value of each tranche on vesting. UK Sub must also account for employer Class 1 National Insurance contributions (NICs) at 13.8%. UK Sub should review whether the employee agreement includes a provision for the employee to meet their own NICs (a "joint election" under ITEPA 2003 s.431) — if so, the NICs liability transfers to the employee.

**Conclusion**

UK-resident employees holding RSUs under the LTIP will be subject to UK income tax under ITEPA 2003, Part 7 on the market value of shares received on each vesting date. UK Sub must operate PAYE on each tranche. The charge arises tranche-by-tranche as restrictions lift, not at the original award date.

**Confidence level: High.** This treatment is well-established for vanilla unvested RSUs subject only to employment conditions. The primary assumption on which this conclusion depends is that the parent shares are readily convertible assets — if they are not, PAYE is not required (though the income tax charge remains). The client should confirm listed-share status with the payroll team.

**Next Steps**

1. Confirm with payroll that PAYE is being operated on each RSU vesting event and that share valuation data is being obtained from the parent company on a monthly basis.
2. Review employee RSU agreements for s.431 joint elections on NICs; ensure elections are signed before vesting if the Company wishes to transfer NICs to employees.
3. Confirm that the ERS annual return (Form 42) has been filed for the LTIP for each relevant tax year — filing is due 6 July following the relevant 5 April.
4. Retain this note and the factual information provided by the client as part of the tax compliance working papers.

*Disclaimer: This note is a template prepared for illustrative purposes. It does not constitute tax advice and must be reviewed by a qualified tax adviser before being relied upon. The analysis applies to UK tax law as at May 2026.*

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Question is stated precisely and is specifically answerable
- [ ] Facts section clearly distinguishes confirmed facts from assumptions
- [ ] Every assumption is explicitly labelled as an assumption
- [ ] Analysis references specific statutory provisions with section numbers
- [ ] Analysis addresses alternative interpretations or risks — not just the preferred view
- [ ] Conclusion directly and unambiguously answers the stated question
- [ ] Confidence level is stated with a brief explanation
- [ ] Conditions on which the conclusion depends are identified
- [ ] Next Steps covers documentation, filing obligations, and any further action
- [ ] British English used throughout
- [ ] Professional disclaimer is included

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Vague question at the top | Analysis lacks a clear endpoint; conclusion cannot be properly stated | State the question as a specific legal question answerable with "yes," "no," or "the charge is X" |
| Facts and assumptions mixed together | Reader cannot tell what is confirmed and what is assumed | Use two sub-sections or flag each assumption explicitly |
| Analysis without statutory references | Not professionally defensible; appears to be opinion not law | Cite specific statutory provisions and guidance documents |
| Conclusion stated without confidence level | Client does not understand the degree of certainty | Always include High / Medium / Low with brief explanation |
| No conditions stated | Client acts on the conclusion when the facts have changed | State the conditions: "this conclusion holds provided that…" |
| Next Steps missing | Client does not know what to do | Always include documentation, filing, and action steps |
| Jargon-heavy for non-specialist audience | Finance director cannot follow the analysis | Match technical register to the stated audience |
