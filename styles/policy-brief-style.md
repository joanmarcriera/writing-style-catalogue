# Policy Brief Style

## What This Style Is

Structured analysis of policy issues. Presents problem, evidence, and recommendations for decision-makers or stakeholders. Objective and evidence-based.

**Core principle**: Issue → Evidence → Options → Recommendation.

## When to Use It

- Government or institutional policy analysis
- Regulatory impact assessments
- Board policy recommendations
- Stakeholder briefings on policy matters
- Research-informed advocacy
- Strategic policy guidance

## When NOT to Use It

- Quick updates (use Smart Brevity)
- Technical specifications (use Technical Documentation)
- Narrative content (use Storytelling)
- Formal legal documents (use Legal Professional)

## Core Structure

```
[ISSUE/PROBLEM]
The policy question or challenge.

[EVIDENCE]
Research, data, or precedent informing analysis.

[OPTIONS]
2–3 policy approaches and their implications.

[ANALYSIS OF OPTIONS]
Pros, cons, and trade-offs of each.

[RECOMMENDATION]
Which approach and why.

[IMPLEMENTATION]
How to execute; stakeholder implications.
```

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a policy analyst. Write a policy brief from:

POLICY ISSUE:
[The question or challenge to address]

CONTEXT:
[Why this matters now]

KEY EVIDENCE:
[Research or data informing this]

POLICY OPTIONS:
[2–3 approaches being considered]

IMPACTS:
[Who is affected and how]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Define the policy issue clearly
2. Present evidence objectively
3. Outline distinct policy options
4. Analyse trade-offs of each option
5. Recommend one approach with rationale
6. Consider stakeholder impact

Output only the policy brief. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Remote Work Policy – Board Brief**

**Issue**
Should we formalize a hybrid or remote-first work policy? Currently, practice is informal and inconsistent across teams.

**Evidence**
- Employee surveys show 73% prefer hybrid or remote arrangements
- Productivity data shows no decline in remote-first teams
- Recruitment: ability to hire remotely expands candidate pool by 3x
- Facility costs: current office utilisation is 45% on average

**Policy Options**

*Option A: Office-First (4 days/week minimum)*
Emphasises collaboration and culture. Requires all employees to commute regularly.

*Option B: Hybrid-Flexible (2 days/week minimum)*
Balances collaboration with autonomy. Employees choose which days.

*Option C: Remote-First*
Defaults to remote; office is optional. Requires strong async communication.

**Analysis**

Option A preserves in-person culture but conflicts with employee preferences and recruitment constraints. Option B satisfies most employees and retains some collaboration benefits. Option C maximises recruitment flexibility and employee autonomy but requires significant process changes.

**Recommendation**
Adopt Option B (Hybrid-Flexible). This balances employee preferences, operational efficiency, and culture.

Implementation: 2 in-person "core days" (Tuesday/Thursday); all other days employee choice.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Issue is clearly defined
- [ ] Evidence is cited or referenced
- [ ] Options are distinct and viable
- [ ] Analysis shows trade-offs objectively
- [ ] Recommendation is justified
- [ ] Stakeholder impact is considered
- [ ] Tone is objective and analytical

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Biased toward one option | Analysis lacks credibility | Present all options objectively first |
| Insufficient evidence | Recommendations feel arbitrary | Ground in data or research |
| Option analysis unclear | Reader can't compare | Use structured format for each option |
| Stakeholder impact missing | Policy seems disconnected from reality | Explain who is affected and how |
