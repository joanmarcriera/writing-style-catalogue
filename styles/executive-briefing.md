# Executive Briefing

## What This Style Is

A formal, polished summary for C-level executives. Balanced and comprehensive. Includes context, analysis, and recommendation with explicit risk assessment.

**Core principle**: Inform and advise at the highest level.

## When to Use It

- Board briefings
- C-suite decision materials
- Formal strategic summaries
- High-stakes proposals
- Investor or stakeholder briefs
- Annual reviews or strategy updates

## When NOT to Use It

- Quick updates (use Smart Brevity)
- Detailed technical specs (use Technical Documentation)
- Teaching or explanation (use Plain English)
- Detailed business case (use Consulting Style or Minto)

## Core Structure

```
[TITLE]

[EXECUTIVE SUMMARY – 1 PARAGRAPH]
The core issue and recommendation.

[BACKGROUND]
Context and why this matters.

[ANALYSIS]
Key findings and supporting data.

[RECOMMENDATION]
What you recommend and why.

[RISKS AND MITIGATION]
What could go wrong and how you'll manage it.

[NEXT STEPS]
Timeline and decision required.
```

```
1. Write in formal but clear language
2. Include one-paragraph executive summary
3. Back all claims with data or analysis
4. Assess risks explicitly
5. Provide clear next steps and timeline
6. Length: 2–4 pages

Output a formal Executive Briefing. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Cloud Infrastructure Migration – Executive Briefing**

**Executive Summary**
We recommend migrating our on-premises infrastructure to AWS over the next 18 months. This reduces capital expenditure by £2.1M, improves system reliability from 98.5% to 99.9%, and unlocks new capabilities for product expansion. Key risk is project delivery; we'll manage this with external migration support.

**Background**
Our current data centre lease expires in 36 months. Renewal costs would be £3.6M over 5 years, plus ongoing maintenance. AWS provides better economics, elasticity, and security compliance for our growth trajectory.

**Key Findings**
• Capital savings: £2.1M over 5 years (lease vs. AWS operating costs)
• Reliability improvement: 98.5% → 99.9% (AWS SLA vs. current performance)
• Time-to-market: 40% faster deployments on cloud-native architecture
• Compliance: AWS meets all GDPR, SOC 2 requirements currently unmet on-premises

**Risks and Mitigation**
• Migration delay risk: Hire external partner; allocate dedicated internal team (£150k)
• Technical debt: Phase migration; maintain parallel systems for 6 months (cost absorbed)

**Recommendation**
Approve £1.2M Phase 1 budget for planning and pilot migration (Q3 2026).

**Next Steps**
Deliver detailed implementation plan by end Q2. Board decision required by June 30.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Executive summary is one paragraph, captures all essentials
- [ ] Recommendation is explicit and supported
- [ ] Data and findings are cited with numbers
- [ ] Risks are identified and mitigation is proposed
- [ ] Timeline is clear and decision point is explicit
- [ ] Tone is formal but accessible
- [ ] Length is appropriate (2–4 pages)
- [ ] No vague language or hedging

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Recommendation buried | Executives need to find the ask quickly | State recommendation in executive summary |
| Vague risks | Executive can't assess true exposure | Name specific risks with mitigation |
| Missing data | Recommendation lacks credibility | Support with numbers and analysis |
| Too much detail too soon | Violates executive briefing format | Lead with summary; detail in sections |
| No timeline | Unclear when decision is needed | Always state decision deadline |
| Passive analysis | Sounds uncertain | Use strong verbs: "This will" not "This could" |
