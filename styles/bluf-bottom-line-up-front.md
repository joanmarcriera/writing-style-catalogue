# BLUF – Bottom Line Up Front

## What This Style Is

BLUF puts the answer, recommendation, or key message in the first sentence. Context, details, and supporting evidence follow. Readers get clarity immediately and can skip details if time is short.

**Core principle**: Answer first, explain second.

## When to Use It

- Emails requiring action or decision
- Short memos
- Status updates requiring a decision
- Problem statements needing response
- Crisis communications
- Internal announcements
- Any document under one page

## When NOT to Use It

- Complex recommendations (use Minto Pyramid for "why")
- Teaching or explanation (use Socratic or Plain English)
- Formal documentation (use Board Paper or Technical Documentation)
- Narrative or storytelling (use Narrative Storytelling)
- Building a logical case (use Consulting Style)

## Core Structure

```
[BLUF – BOTTOM LINE IN FIRST SENTENCE]
The clear, actionable answer or recommendation.

[CONTEXT]
Why this is needed.

[KEY POINTS]
Supporting facts, decisions, or rationale.

[ACTION REQUIRED]
What the reader needs to do and when.

[NEXT STEPS OR QUESTIONS]
Who to contact; when follow-up happens.
```

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a BLUF expert. Write an email or memo in BLUF style from the following brief:

SITUATION:
[Describe the situation or problem]

YOUR RECOMMENDATION OR ANSWER:
[What you recommend or conclude]

SUPPORTING FACTS:
[List 3–5 facts that support your answer]

ACTION REQUIRED:
[What you need the reader to do]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. First sentence must be the bottom line (recommendation or answer)
2. Keep total length under 300 words
3. Use bullet points for supporting facts
4. End with clear action and deadline
5. Use professional but conversational tone

Format:

**Subject: [Clear subject line indicating your ask]**

[BLUF – one sentence with your answer or recommendation]

[2–3 sentences of context – why this matters]

**Supporting facts:**
• Fact 1
• Fact 2
• Fact 3

**Action required:**
[What you need and by when]

Output only the email/memo. No preamble.
```

## Short Example

**Subject: Approval Needed – Supplier Contract Extension**

We should approve the contract extension with TechVendor Ltd. This locks in current pricing for 24 months and avoids a 15% increase if we renegotiate next quarter.

Background: Our current contract expires in Q3. The vendor has offered a 24-month extension at existing rates (£85k/month) through end of 2027. If we allow the contract to expire, industry pricing typically increases 10–15% on renewal.

**Supporting facts:**
• Current spend: £85k/month
• Renewal penalty if we wait: 15% increase (£97.75k/month)
• Commitment period: 24 months
• No service degradation; existing terms remain identical

**Action required:**
Please approve the extension by Friday 17 May. I'll execute the contract immediately upon approval.

**Questions?** Contact me or reach the finance team.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] First sentence contains the bottom line
- [ ] Bottom line is actionable or clear
- [ ] Context is concise (1–3 sentences)
- [ ] Supporting facts are bulleted, not paragraphs
- [ ] Action and deadline are explicit
- [ ] Total length under 300 words
- [ ] No hedging language ("seems," "quite," "relatively")
- [ ] Tone is professional but accessible

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Burying the bottom line | Reader has to hunt for the answer | Put the answer in sentence one |
| Too much context before the answer | Delays clarity | Front-load the bottom line, then explain |
| Vague action items | Reader unsure what to do | "Approve by Friday" not "Let me know your thoughts" |
| No deadline | Action may never happen | Always include a specific date |
| Too many supporting facts | Overwhelms reader | Keep to 3–5 max |
| Weak language | Sounds unsure | Use "Should" or "Must" not "Might want to consider" |
| Missing numbers | Impact is unclear | "Saves £50k" not "Significant savings" |
