# Social Media Style

## What This Style Is

Punchy, engaging writing for social platforms. Conversational and authentic. Designed for scanning and sharing, not deep reading.

**Core principle**: Hook → Point → Engagement.

## When to Use It

- LinkedIn posts and updates
- Twitter/X posts
- Company announcements via social
- Thought leadership shares
- Product launches
- Community engagement

## When NOT to Use It

- Formal business communication (use Board Paper)
- Technical documentation (use Technical Documentation)
- Crisis communication (use Crisis Communications)
- Persuasive recommendations (use Consulting)

## Platform Considerations

**LinkedIn**: Professional, longer form (up to 3000 chars), focus on insights and expertise.

**Twitter/X**: Concise (280 chars), punchy, conversational, link-friendly.

**Internal Slack**: Casual, quick, authentic, emoji-friendly.

## Core Principles

- **Hook first**: First 1–2 sentences must compel reading
- **Concise**: Cut every unnecessary word
- **Authentic**: Sound like a real person, not corporate
- **Scannable**: Use line breaks; not dense paragraphs
- **Engaging**: Ask questions; invite response

## Copy-Paste Prompt Template

```
You are a social media writer. Write a post from:

PLATFORM:
[LinkedIn / Twitter / Slack]

TOPIC:
[What are you sharing]

KEY POINT:
[What do you want people to know]

CONTEXT:
[Why this matters; why now]

CALL TO ACTION:
[What should people do: like, share, comment, click]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Lead with hook (first line must grab attention)
2. Use line breaks; not dense paragraphs
3. Keep language conversational
4. Stay authentic; avoid corporate-speak
5. For LinkedIn: can be longer; focus on insight
6. For Twitter: punchy; under 280 chars ideally
7. Include CTA (question, ask to share, link)

Output only the post. No preamble.
```

## Short Examples

**LinkedIn Post**

Seven years ago I thought you needed a data warehouse to be a data company.

Wrong.

We were stuck with a £4M/year bill for tech we barely used. The real work—understanding what the data means—happened in spreadsheets.

When we switched to a simpler, query-able system:
• Cost dropped to £600k/year
• Time to insight dropped from 2 weeks to 2 days
• Our team actually used the data

The lesson: complexity often isn't sophistication. Sometimes simplicity wins.

What's the most overengineered system you've worked with?

---

**Twitter Post**

PostgreSQL won me over the moment I realised I could write a single query to answer a question that would've taken 3 hours in Mongo.

What's your favorite database unpopular opinion?

---

**Slack Post**

🎉 Just shipped the new analytics dashboard! 

It's faster, the UI doesn't make your eyes hurt, and you can actually export data now.

Grab it here: [link]

Hit me with bugs or feedback—[@channel]

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] First line hooks the reader
- [ ] Message is clear in 5 seconds
- [ ] Language is conversational, not corporate
- [ ] Includes a call to action (comment, share, click)
- [ ] Breaks are used for readability
- [ ] On-brand and authentic
- [ ] Platform-appropriate length

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Weak opening | Reader scrolls past | Lead with the surprising or compelling point |
| Corporate tone | Feels disconnected; less engagement | Write like a human, not a brand |
| Dense paragraphs | Hard to read on mobile | Use line breaks; max 2–3 sentences per line |
| No CTA | Low engagement | Ask a question or invite action |
| Too long for platform | Gets cut off or ignored | LinkedIn allows length; Twitter doesn't |
| No personality | Sounds like an ad | Show your perspective; be opinionated |
