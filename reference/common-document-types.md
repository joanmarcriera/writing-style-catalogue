## Style by decision urgency

**Decision needed within hours**
→ BLUF or Smart Brevity — the reader needs the answer in the first sentence; they do not have time to read for context first

**Decision needed this week**
→ Minto Pyramid or Executive Briefing — enough space to present the analysis; conclusion still leads

**Decision needed this month**
→ Board Paper, Business Case, or Tax Technical Note — formal, comprehensive, internally reviewed before submission

**Decision needed this quarter (programme planning)**
→ Roadmap, Risk Register, or Strategy Document — captures a plan rather than seeking an immediate decision

**No decision — for knowledge or audit record**
→ ADR, SOP, Runbook, Data Room Index — reference documents; accuracy and completeness matter more than conciseness

## Document type by organisational context

### IT and cloud infrastructure

| Situation | Document to produce |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Justifying a major infrastructure investment | Business case + slide deck |
| Documenting a technical architectural decision | ADR |
| Creating operational procedure for on-call team | Runbook or SOP |
| Communicating cloud migration to leadership | Board paper or executive briefing |
| Training staff on new cloud platform | Training material |
| Assessing IAM vendors | Comparison table |
| Planning a multi-phase programme | Roadmap + risk register |

### Tax and professional advisory

| Situation | Document to produce |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Answering a specific client tax question | Tax technical note |
| Briefing a non-specialist client on a tax position | Executive memo or one-page brief |
| Preparing for an HMRC enquiry | Tax technical note + file note |
| Comparing structuring options for a transaction | Comparison table + tax technical note |
| Summarising advice for a board | One-page brief |

### Executive and board communication

| Situation | Document to produce |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Seeking board approval for major spend | Board paper |
| Quarterly performance update | Executive update (Smart Brevity) |
| Briefing a new board member | One-page brief or executive memo |
| Responding to an investor's question | Executive memo |
| Announcing a major change | Executive memo + staff communication |

## Length rules of thumb

- **One-pagers**: If it does not fit on one page at 11pt with 2.5cm margins, cut content — do not reduce margins or font size
- **Memos**: Two pages maximum. If you need three pages, you need a board paper, not a memo
- **Board papers**: 8–12 pages excluding appendices. A 5-page board paper lacks evidence; a 20-page board paper will not be read in full
- **Runbooks**: No length limit, but every word must earn its place — users are under pressure when they read these
- **Training modules**: 400–600 words of explanation per module; beyond that, split the module
