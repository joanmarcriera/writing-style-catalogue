# Workflow: Job Application Process

A strong job application is not a summary of your career — it is a targeted argument for why you are the best candidate for this specific role. Every element (cover letter, CV, LinkedIn profile) must be calibrated to the job description, the organisation, and the specific hiring manager's priorities. Generic applications are immediately recognisable and rarely advance. This workflow takes you from reading the job description to submitting a polished, personalised application in under a day of focused work.

## Style to use

[Job Application Style](../styles/job-application-style.md) — direct, confident, and evidence-led. The cover letter should lead with the most compelling reason you are right for this role, not with "I am writing to apply for the position of..." Use [BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)](../styles/bluf-bottom-line-up-front.md) structure: your strongest point in the opening sentence, evidence in the body, clear close.

## Skill to use

[Create One-Page Brief](../skills/create-one-page-brief.md) — a cover letter is a one-page brief making the case for your candidacy. [Create Email Sequence](../skills/create-email-sequence.md) — use this for a follow-up sequence if you have not heard back within 2 weeks.

## Inputs required

Before drafting, gather:
- The full job description — read it three times; underline every specific requirement
- The organisation's website, recent news, annual report, or press releases (understand what they care about)
- The name and role of the hiring manager if publicly available (LinkedIn)
- Your 3–5 most relevant achievements — with specific numbers and outcomes, not just responsibilities
- The specific reasons this role appeals to you (beyond salary and title — what about the organisation, team, or problem?)
- Your CV, updated to reflect this application

## Copy-paste master prompt

```
You are a career coach and professional writer. Write a compelling cover letter for the job application described below.

AUDIENCE: [Hiring manager — name and title if known; otherwise role and organisation]
PURPOSE: To make a compelling, specific case for why I am the strongest candidate for this role.
CONTEXT: [Role title and organisation; how I found the role; any mutual connections or referrals]
INPUT:
Job description highlights: [3–5 specific requirements from the JD that I meet strongly]
My strongest relevant achievements:
- [Achievement 1: what I did, how, and the measurable outcome]
- [Achievement 2: what I did, how, and the measurable outcome]
- [Achievement 3: what I did, how, and the measurable outcome]
Why this role / organisation: [Specific, genuine reasons — not generic "exciting opportunity" language]
Any gaps to address: [e.g. I do not have experience in X; here is why that does not matter / how I will address it]
DESIRED_OUTCOME: The hiring manager reads this letter and immediately wants to interview me.
TONE: Confident and direct, but not arrogant. Warm enough to feel human; professional enough for the context. Match the organisation's culture if visible.
LENGTH: One page maximum. 350–450 words.
FORMAT: Opening paragraph (the single strongest reason I am right for this role — not an introduction). Middle paragraphs: 2–3 specific examples with measurable outcomes. Closing paragraph: why this organisation specifically and the clear next step.
CONSTRAINTS: Use British English. No hollow opening phrases: "I am writing to express my interest in...", "I was excited to see..." — open with a statement of value. Every claim must be backed by a specific example or number. Output only the cover letter; no preamble.
```

## Step-by-step process

1. **Research** (45 minutes): Read the job description carefully — print it and underline every requirement. Research the organisation: read the website, look for recent news, check their LinkedIn page and the hiring manager's profile. What does the organisation care about? What problems are they trying to solve? Note 3 specific facts you can reference.

2. **Identify your best evidence** (30 minutes): Select 3–5 achievements that most directly map to the role requirements. For each, frame it as: situation → what you did → measurable outcome. Reject vague achievements ("led a team") in favour of specific ones ("led a team of 8 engineers through an 18-month Azure migration, delivered on time and £120k under budget").

3. **Draft the cover letter** (30 minutes): Use the master prompt above. Populate all INPUT fields. Do not send the first draft — read it critically. Does the opening sentence make a specific claim? Are the examples concrete? Is the "why this organisation" paragraph specific and genuine?

4. **Tailor your CV** (45 minutes): Move your most relevant experience to the top. Adjust the summary to reflect this specific role. Check that the skills and keywords in the JD appear in your CV — not to trick keyword scanners, but because they signal alignment.

5. **Review** (20 minutes): Use the review checklist below. Ask: would I be compelled to interview this person? If not, what is missing?

6. **Submit** (10 minutes): Follow the application instructions precisely. If asked for a covering letter as a PDF, provide a PDF. If a portal requires pasting text, ensure formatting survives. Keep a record: organisation, role, date, contact.

7. **Follow up** (if needed, 2 weeks after submission): A single, brief, polite follow-up email is acceptable if you have not received any acknowledgement. Use [Create Email Sequence](../skills/create-email-sequence.md) for a structured follow-up approach.

**Total time: approximately 3–4 hours per application**

## Review checklist

- [ ] Opening sentence makes a specific claim — not a generic introduction
- [ ] Every achievement claim has a specific number or measurable outcome
- [ ] "Why this organisation" paragraph references something specific about the organisation — not a generic statement
- [ ] The cover letter is one page or under at standard font size (11pt, 2.5cm margins)
- [ ] CV is tailored: most relevant experience is prominent; skills in the JD appear in the CV
- [ ] Spelling and grammar are perfect — use a spell-checker and read aloud
- [ ] British English throughout; no Americanisms
- [ ] Application submitted according to the instructions (correct file format, correct portal, correct contact)

## Example output excerpt

> Three years leading IT transformation programmes for FTSE 250 clients is directly relevant to the Head of Technology Delivery role at Meridian Financial. In that time I have delivered four major cloud migrations, two ERP implementations, and a full IAM programme — consistently on time and within 5% of budget.
>
> The Azure migration I led for a 1,200-person financial services firm is the closest parallel to your current infrastructure programme. We migrated 140 servers across 3 data centres in 14 months, achieving FCA operational resilience compliance and reducing annual infrastructure costs by £680k. The critical success factors were stakeholder alignment at board level, a phased approach with clear go/no-go gates, and a dedicated change management workstream.
>
> I am drawn to Meridian specifically because of your stated commitment to achieving ISO 27001 certification by 2026 — that is a programme I know how to deliver, and your infrastructure complexity (multi-cloud, hybrid, regulated) is exactly the environment where my experience adds most value.
