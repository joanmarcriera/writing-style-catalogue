# Workflow: Supplier or Importer Outreach

Supplier and importer outreach is a commercial prospecting activity: you are asking a business to invest time in a conversation with you, before you have established any value or relationship. The emails must be short (under 150 words), specific (reference something real about their business), and lead with the benefit to them — not a description of you. Most outreach fails because it is long, generic, and self-focused. This workflow builds a personalised, sequenced outreach campaign that earns responses and converts them into productive commercial conversations.

## Style to use

[Persuasive Sales Style](../styles/persuasive-sales-style.md) — concise, benefit-led, and specific. The opening line should reference something specific about the prospect's business (a product line, a market they serve, a challenge their sector faces). Do not use marketing language ("cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "game-changing") — it destroys credibility immediately with professional buyers.

## Inputs required

Before starting, gather for each prospect:
- Company name, size, and key product lines
- The name and role of the decision-maker you are targeting
- A specific, genuine observation about their business (from their website, LinkedIn, trade press, or a mutual contact)
- The specific value you offer them — quantified if possible ("our cold-chain logistics software reduces spoilage by an average of 18%")
- The ask: what is the first step you want them to take? (A 20-minute call is better than a long meeting)
- Any sector challenges they are likely facing (post-Brexit complexity, energy costs, regulatory changes)

## Copy-paste master prompt

```
You are a professional copywriter specialising in B2B commercial outreach. Write a 4-email outreach sequence targeting the prospect described below.

AUDIENCE: [Prospect role, company type, sector — e.g. Operations Director at a UK food importer with 50–200 employees]
PURPOSE: To open a commercial conversation leading to a discovery call where we can present our offering in detail.
CONTEXT: [Your company and offering; the specific value proposition relevant to this prospect; any sector context (challenges, trends, regulatory environment) that makes your timing relevant]
INPUT:
What we offer: [Specific product/service; quantified benefit where possible]
Prospect's likely pain points: [What they are struggling with in their sector right now]
Specific observation: [Something real and specific about their business — a product line, a market, a recent announcement]
First ask: [The low-friction first step — typically a 20-minute call]
DESIRED_OUTCOME: A reply to any one of the four emails that leads to a scheduled discovery call.
TONE: Direct, professional, and specific. No marketing language. No hollow openers. Write as a peer, not as a salesperson.
LENGTH: Email 1: 100–130 words. Email 2: 80–100 words. Email 3: 100–120 words. Email 4 (close): 60–80 words.
FORMAT: For each email: send timing; two subject line options; full body copy; one clear CTA.
CONSTRAINTS: Use British English. Every email must feel individually readable — assume the reader has not seen the previous one. No email should use "I hope this finds you well", "Following up on my previous email", or "I wanted to reach out." Output only the sequence; no preamble.

SEQUENCE STRATEGY:
Email 1 (Day 1): Specific insight about their sector/business + concise value prop + one question as CTA (not a meeting request)
Email 2 (Day 4): Add value (a data point, case study, or tool relevant to them) before asking for anything
Email 3 (Day 10): Different angle; address the most likely objection; ask for the call
Email 4 (Day 18): Brief, respectful close; leave the door open; no pressure
```

## Step-by-step process

1. **Build your prospect list** (2–3 hours for a batch of 20 prospects): Identify prospects through industry directories, trade associations, LinkedIn company search, trade show exhibitor lists, or referrals from existing contacts. For each prospect, confirm: the company exists and is active, the decision-maker is identifiable, and there is a plausible reason they might need what you offer.

2. **Research each prospect individually** (20–30 minutes per prospect): Visit their website. Read their LinkedIn company page. Check for recent news, new product launches, or market entries. Note one specific, genuine observation you can use in your Email 1 opening line. This is the step most outreach skips — it is the reason most outreach fails.

3. **Draft the email sequence** (30 minutes per prospect, or 1–2 hours for a templated sequence): Use the master prompt above. Populate the INPUT fields for each prospect. For a large prospect list (50+), draft a template sequence for each prospect type (e.g. food importer, pharmaceutical importer, industrial goods importer) and personalise the opening line for each individual.

4. **Send Email 1** and set calendar reminders for Email 2, 3, and 4: Do not rely on memory or a spreadsheet — use your email client's scheduling function or a CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, or even Outlook's send later) to schedule follow-ups at the right intervals.

5. **Manage responses** (ongoing): Respond to any reply within 24 hours. A positive response ("happy to chat") should go straight to a calendar booking — do not let it go cold. A "not the right time" response should be noted with a follow-up date in 3–6 months.

6. **Meetings and discovery** (when response received): Prepare a structured discovery agenda using [Create Meeting Pack](../skills/create-meeting-pack.md). Bring the one-page capability brief created with [Create One-Page Brief](../skills/create-one-page-brief.md). Take notes on their specific needs for use in the proposal.

7. **Review and optimise** (after every 20 sends): Check open rates and reply rates. If Email 1 open rates are under 30%, rewrite the subject line. If reply rates are under 5%, rewrite the Email 1 opening line and CTA. Iterate — outreach improves with each round.

**Total time: 3–4 hours to set up a 20-prospect campaign; 30–60 minutes per week to manage responses**

## Review checklist

- [ ] Every Email 1 opening line references something specific and real about the prospect's business
- [ ] The value proposition is quantified — not "we save you money" but "we reduce spoilage by an average of 18%"
- [ ] No email exceeds 150 words of body copy
- [ ] The CTA escalates correctly — Email 1 asks a question; Email 3 asks for the call
- [ ] Subject lines are under 50 characters and do not sound like marketing
- [ ] British English throughout; no Americanisms
- [ ] Calendar reminders set for Email 2, 3, and 4 sends for every active prospect
- [ ] A spreadsheet or CRM is tracking: prospect, email sent, date, response, next action

>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Your ambient-to-chilled product range caught my attention — it is exactly the area where UK importers are seeing the most spoilage pressure post-Brexit, with customs dwell times extending average cold-chain exposure by 18–22%.
>
> We work with food importers to reduce spoilage through real-time temperature monitoring and predictive routing. Clients typically see a 15–20% reduction in spoilage-related write-offs within the first 6 months.
>
> Quick question: is cold-chain spoilage something your team is actively working to reduce, or is it currently accepted as a cost of business?
>
> [Your name]
