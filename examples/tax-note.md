# Tax Note Example

**Illustrative only:** This example shows structure and drafting style. It is not tax advice. Statutory references, HMRC guidance, and case law must be verified by a qualified tax adviser before use.

## Tax Technical Note: VAT Treatment of Cross-Border SaaS Supply

**Client / Matter:** Internal draft for Finance Director
**Date:** 14 May 2026
**Status:** Draft for adviser review
**Jurisdiction:** UK VAT

## 1. Question

Is the monthly subscription fee charged by a UK company to a German VAT-registered business customer for access to a software-as-a-service platform within the scope of UK VAT, or should it be treated as outside the scope with the customer accounting for VAT under the reverse charge mechanism?

## 2. Facts and Assumptions

The following facts have been provided:

1. Supplier: UK-established company.
2. Customer: German-established business, VAT registered in Germany.
3. Supply: subscription access to a hosted software platform.
4. Contract: business-to-business subscription, billed monthly.
5. Customer has provided a German VAT number.
6. No on-site installation or physical goods are supplied.

Assumptions:

- The customer is acting in the course of business and has provided a valid VAT registration number.
- The service is an electronically supplied service and not a supply of consultancy, training, or bespoke development.
- No fixed establishment of the customer in the UK receives the service.

## 3. Legal Framework

For a UK VAT analysis, the relevant starting point is the place-of-supply rules for services and HMRC's published guidance on business-to-business supplies of digital services. Exact statutory references and HMRC manual references should be inserted and verified by the adviser before the note is finalised.

For business-to-business services, the general rule is that the place of supply is where the customer belongs, unless a specific exception applies. Where the customer belongs outside the UK, the supply is generally outside the scope of UK VAT, and the customer may be required to account for VAT in its jurisdiction under the reverse charge mechanism.

## 4. Analysis

The supply is access to a hosted software platform, provided electronically and billed as a recurring subscription. On the facts provided, this is a service rather than a supply of goods.

The customer is established in Germany and has provided a German VAT registration number. On that basis, the customer should be treated as a business customer for place-of-supply purposes. No facts suggest that the service is received by a UK fixed establishment, and no specific exception has been identified from the information provided.

Accordingly, the place of supply is expected to be Germany rather than the UK. The UK supplier should not charge UK VAT on the invoice, provided the customer's VAT status has been verified and appropriate evidence is retained. The invoice should include wording indicating that the reverse charge applies, using the organisation's approved invoice wording.

The conclusion depends on the customer genuinely acting as a business and the supply being correctly characterised as a digital subscription service. If the customer is not VAT registered, is receiving the service for non-business purposes, or receives the service through a UK establishment, the analysis may change.

## 5. Conclusion

On the facts and assumptions above, the monthly SaaS subscription should be treated as outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is expected to be Germany. The German business customer should account for VAT under the reverse charge mechanism in Germany.

**Confidence level: Medium to high**, subject to verification of the customer's VAT number, confirmation that no UK fixed establishment receives the service, and adviser verification of the statutory and HMRC guidance references.

## 6. Next Steps

1. Verify the customer's German VAT number and retain evidence of the check.
2. Confirm that the customer has no UK fixed establishment receiving the service.
3. Confirm the approved reverse charge wording for invoices.
4. Ask a qualified VAT adviser to insert and verify the precise statutory references and HMRC guidance before relying on this note.
5. Retain the contract, VAT number evidence, and invoice wording as VAT compliance records.

## Why this example works

- The question is narrow and answerable.
- Facts and assumptions are separate.
- The analysis is conditional and does not overstate certainty.
- Missing legal references are flagged for qualified review rather than invented.
- Next steps tell the finance team what to verify and retain.
