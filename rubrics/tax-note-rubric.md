# Tax Note Rubric

Use this for tax technical notes, adviser memos, and tax analysis produced with LLM support.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | Topic only | Question present but broad | Single precise tax question that can be answered from the facts |
| Facts | Facts mixed with analysis | Material facts mostly captured | Confirmed facts, assumptions, dates, amounts, entities, and jurisdiction separated |
| Law and guidance | Missing or generic | Some references present | Specific statutes, HMRC guidance, and cases supplied by the adviser are applied accurately |
| Analysis | Jumps to conclusion | Basic application | Step-by-step application of law to facts, including alternatives |
| Confidence | Over-confident or absent | Confidence stated | Confidence level explained with drivers of uncertainty |
| Caveats | Boilerplate only | Some limitations | Material conditions, missing facts, and change-in-law risks clearly identified |
| Next steps | Missing or vague | Generic actions | Documentation, filings, clearances, disclosures, and review actions specified |
| Professional safety | Relies on LLM as adviser | Adviser review mentioned | Clearly requires qualified review and independent verification before reliance |

## Pass standard

A tax note generated by an LLM is never client-ready without qualified human review. For internal drafting, require average score **4** and no score below **3**.

## Red flags

- Invented statutory references, HMRC manual numbers, or case citations.
- No distinction between confirmed facts and assumptions.
- Conclusion states certainty where the facts or law do not support it.
- The note gives filing or disclosure advice without verifying current law.
