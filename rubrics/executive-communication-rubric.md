# Executive Communication Rubric

Use this for executive briefings, CIO/CFO updates, approval emails, and senior stakeholder memos.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bottom line | Buried or missing | Present but not first or not decisive | First paragraph states the answer, ask, or recommendation clearly |
| Audience fit | Generic | Some senior-level framing | Written for the actual reader's decision rights, concerns, and time pressure |
| Evidence | Vague assertions | Some figures but limited basis | Key claims backed by specific facts, figures, or named internal sources |
| Decision clarity | No explicit ask | Ask present but weak | Decision, owner, deadline, and consequence of delay are explicit |
| Risk and caveats | Risks hidden or minimised | Risks mentioned | Material risks, caveats, and mitigations are named plainly |
| Brevity | Too long or padded | Reasonable but uneven | Tight, skimmable, and no filler |
| Tone | Too casual, defensive, or over-polished | Professional but bland | Direct, measured, confident, and in British English |

## Pass standard

Minimum professional threshold: average score **4** and no criterion below **3**.

## Red flags

- The reader cannot tell what decision is required.
- The recommendation has no owner or date.
- The language hides uncertainty instead of naming it.
- Numbers appear without source, basis, or caveat.
