# Slide Deck Rubric

Use this for board, executive, client, investor, or internal presentation outlines.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative | Sequence of topics | Basic story arc | Clear flow from problem to evidence to recommendation to action |
| Slide titles | Topic labels | Mixed labels and claims | Each title states the slide's message as a sentence |
| Density | Text-heavy | Mostly readable | One idea per slide, no more than 3-4 bullets, detail in notes |
| Evidence | Claims unsupported | Some data included | Data, assumptions, and caveats are visible and source-aware |
| Visuals | Generic "add chart" | Some useful visual ideas | Each slide has a specific visual spec: chart type, data, emphasis |
| Speaker notes | Missing or shallow | Presenter prompts | Full notes explain what to say, caveats, and transitions |
| Call to action | Ends with "Questions?" | Ask present | Final message states the decision, action, or commitment needed |
| Audience fit | Generic | Reasonable tone | Matched to seniority, technical depth, time slot, and decision context |

## Pass standard

The deck should score **4 or higher** for narrative, slide titles, and call to action before being built in PowerPoint or Google Slides.

## Red flags

- The deck repeats a paper instead of presenting the argument.
- Slides have topic titles such as "Background" or "Financials".
- Speaker notes say "talk about X" rather than giving usable presenter language.
- The final slide is only "Thank you" or "Questions?" with no ask.
