# Checklist: Board Paper Review

Use before a board paper goes to the company secretary, chair, or board portal.

## Decision Readiness

- [ ] The decision required is stated in one precise sentence in the executive summary.
- [ ] The paper says whether the board is asked to approve, note, endorse, ratify, or discuss.
- [ ] Proposed resolution wording is included and suitable for the minutes.
- [ ] The consequence of not deciding now is clear.
- [ ] The board's decision authority is confirmed.

## Options and Recommendation

- [ ] At least two genuine options are presented, plus "do nothing" where relevant.
- [ ] Options are assessed against the same criteria.
- [ ] The recommended option is not favoured by biased language in the options section.
- [ ] The rationale for rejecting alternatives is explicit.
- [ ] Success criteria are measurable and time-bound.

## Financials

- [ ] All financial figures have been reviewed by Finance.
- [ ] Costs, savings, cash flow, and funding source are internally consistent.
- [ ] Time horizon is consistent across options.
- [ ] Assumptions and sensitivities are stated.
- [ ] Any figures not yet approved are labelled as estimates.

## Risk and Governance

- [ ] Material strategic, financial, operational, legal, regulatory, and reputational risks are covered.
- [ ] Each risk has likelihood, impact, mitigation, and owner.
- [ ] Legal, compliance, tax, procurement, or data protection implications have been reviewed by the right specialist.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest or related-party issues are disclosed if relevant.
- [ ] Appendices support the paper without being required to understand the decision.

## Executive Quality

- [ ] Executive summary is one page maximum and stands alone.
- [ ] Headings are informative, not generic.
- [ ] No unexplained acronyms or technical detail that non-executive directors cannot follow.
- [ ] British English used throughout.
- [ ] The paper can be read in the time available to directors.
