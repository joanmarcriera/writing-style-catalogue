# Checklist: Website SEO Audit Review

Use this before sharing a website marketing or SEO audit.

## Scope and Evidence

- [ ] Primary URL and reviewed pages are listed.
- [ ] Data sources are named: public site, analytics, Search Console, CMS, crawler, keyword tool, screenshots, or supplied notes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Observed facts, inferred hypotheses, and missing information are labelled.
- [ ] No private metrics are invented from public inspection alone.

## Marketing and Positioning

- [ ] The homepage explains who the site is for and what the organisation offers.
- [ ] The value proposition is specific, not generic.
- [ ] The main audience, problem, outcome, and proof are clear.
- [ ] Differentiators are supported by evidence.
- [ ] Messaging matches the likely stage of awareness.

## Conversion

- [ ] Each key page has one primary action.
- [ ] Calls to action are visible, specific, and repeated at sensible points.
- [ ] Forms ask only for information needed at that stage.
- [ ] Trust signals, proof, credentials, testimonials, case studies, process, or guarantees are visible where relevant.
- [ ] Objections and risk concerns are addressed.
- [ ] Behavioural claims are backed by analytics, heatmaps, user research, or labelled as hypotheses.

## Technical SEO

- [ ] Pages appear crawlable and indexable where they should be.
- [ ] robots.txt and sitemap are reviewed where available.
- [ ] Titles, meta descriptions, headings, canonicals, and internal links are checked on key pages.
- [ ] Redirect, duplicate, noindex, broken-link, or thin-template risks are noted where observed.
- [ ] Structured data is reviewed where visible.
- [ ] Mobile layout and performance signals are reviewed where tools or screenshots are available.
- [ ] Core Web Vitals are not claimed unless field or lab data is supplied.

## Content and Search Intent

- [ ] Key audience questions are mapped to page types.
- [ ] Commercial, informational, comparison, proof, local, and support content are distinguished.
- [ ] Topic clusters and internal links are recommended where useful.
- [ ] Thin, duplicated, stale, or orphaned content is identified.
- [ ] Keyword volume, difficulty, traffic, or rankings are not invented.
- [ ] Expert, legal, tax, medical, or regulated claims are flagged for review where relevant.

## Measurement and Action Plan

- [ ] The report defines what should be measured next.
- [ ] Critical tracking gaps are listed before experiments.
- [ ] Recommendations are prioritised by impact, effort, confidence, and dependency.
- [ ] Owners are assigned: marketing, content, design, engineering, analytics, or leadership.
- [ ] The 30/60/90-day plan is realistic for the stated capacity.
- [ ] Acceptance criteria are included for technical and content tasks.

## Red Flags

- [ ] The report says "increase traffic" without defining audience, page, intent, or measurement.
- [ ] The report gives a long generic checklist instead of a prioritised plan.
- [ ] It recommends content without explaining the search intent or commercial purpose.
- [ ] It recommends experiments without a baseline or measurement plan.
- [ ] It hides uncertainty instead of saying what data is missing.
