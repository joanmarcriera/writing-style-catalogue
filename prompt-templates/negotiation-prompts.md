# Negotiation Prompts

## Purpose

Effective negotiation communication is collaborative, not adversarial — it focuses on interests (what both parties actually need) rather than positions (what each party initially demands). Use these prompts when you need to write an opening position, a counter-proposal, a response to an unacceptable offer, or a message designed to break a deadlock. The output must be precise about what you are proposing, honest about your constraints, and clear about what you need from the other party. It should never use ultimatums unless you are genuinely prepared to act on them, and it should always leave room for the counterparty to respond constructively. Good negotiation writing moves a conversation forward; bad negotiation writing entrenches positions.

## Copy-paste prompt — Negotiation email

```
You are an experienced commercial negotiator. Write a professional, effective negotiation message that advances the sender's position while keeping the conversation collaborative and constructive.

AUDIENCE: [AUDIENCE — the counterparty, their role and organisation]

PURPOSE: [PURPOSE — what you are negotiating and what stage the negotiation is at]

CONTEXT: [CONTEXT — background to the negotiation: how long it has been ongoing, what has been agreed so far, what the sticking points are]

INPUT (your position, their position, your constraints, your flexibility, and your ideal and acceptable outcomes):
[INPUT]

DESIRED_OUTCOME: [DESIRED_OUTCOME — what you want the counterparty to do after reading this message]

TONE: [TONE — recommended: professional, collaborative, precise — not aggressive or appeasing]

LENGTH: [LENGTH — recommended: 200–400 words for a negotiation email; longer for a formal counter-proposal letter]

FORMAT: [FORMAT — recommended: shared interest or common ground opening / your position with rationale / acknowledgement of their position / proposed path forward or specific trade-off / invitation to respond]

CONSTRAINTS: [CONSTRAINTS]

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Write in British English throughout.
2. Open by referencing the shared interest or common goal — what both parties ultimately want from this negotiation (a successful contract, a long-term partnership, a project delivered on time). This frames the negotiation as problem-solving, not combat.
3. State your position clearly and precisely. Vague positions ("we need more flexibility on price") are not negotiable. Specific positions ("we are seeking a 12% reduction from the proposed day rate, from £1,150 to £1,012") are.
4. Give a brief rationale for your position — not as justification but to help the counterparty understand your constraints and find a path forward. Do not over-explain or justify at length; this appears weak.
5. Acknowledge the counterparty's position genuinely — note what you understand their constraints to be and show that you have considered their perspective. Do not be dismissive.
6. Propose a specific path forward: a trade-off, a package, an alternative structure, or a question that invites them to suggest one. Do not leave the ball in the air — give them something to respond to.
7. Preserve all factual details from the input — do not invent figures, terms, or commitments.
8. Distinguish between: your confirmed position (what you have committed to), your flexibility (areas where movement is possible — describe without revealing your full hand), and your constraints (what genuinely cannot move).
9. Do not use ultimatums unless you are genuinely prepared to act on them. An ultimatum you back down from destroys your credibility in the negotiation.
10. Leave room for the counterparty to respond and maintain their dignity. They need to be able to say yes without feeling defeated.
11. If you are making an assumption about the counterparty's position or constraints, frame it as such.
12. Tone: [TONE].
13. Length: [LENGTH].
14. Apply these constraints: [CONSTRAINTS].
15. Output only the negotiation message. No preamble.
```

## Variables to customise

- `[AUDIENCE]` — the counterparty. Examples: "the commercial director at CloudBridge Ltd, your delivery partner for Phase 2 of the cloud migration", "the hiring manager negotiating the compensation package for a Director of Cloud Operations role", "the account manager at a major SaaS vendor on a contract renewal".
- `[PURPOSE]` — what is being negotiated. Examples: "day rate for specialist cloud engineers on a 6-month engagement", "contract payment terms (net 60 vs. the vendor's proposed net 30)", "salary and benefits package for a new role", "exit terms from an underperforming supplier contract".
- `[CONTEXT]` — the negotiation history. Examples: "the first offer was submitted last week; this is the counter-proposal", "we have been in negotiation for three weeks; this is an attempt to break the deadlock on the milestone payment structure", "the existing contract expires on 31 July; this is the renewal negotiation".
- `[INPUT]` — all the substance: your opening or current position, their position, your ideal outcome, your acceptable outcome, and your genuine constraints (i.e. what you truly cannot move on). The more specific the better.
- `[DESIRED_OUTCOME]` — what the counterparty should do after reading. Examples: "agree to the proposed day rate in writing by 28 May", "come back with a revised package that meets the minimum on salary", "agree to schedule a call to discuss the milestone structure".
- `[TONE]` — "professional, collaborative, precise". Adjust based on relationship: longer-term partners can be slightly warmer; adversarial negotiations may require a more formal register.
- `[LENGTH]` — 200–400 words for an email; 500–800 words for a formal counter-proposal letter.
- `[FORMAT]` — use the recommended structure. For formal counter-proposals, add a summary table of terms if there are multiple line items being negotiated.
- `[CONSTRAINTS]` — examples: "do not reveal the ceiling rate until round 3", "the payment terms constraint is non-negotiable — but this must be communicated without closing the conversation", "this message will be forwarded to their legal team — be precise with language and avoid any commitments not explicitly in the input".

## Example input

```
AUDIENCE: Commercial Director at CloudBridge Ltd — negotiating the engagement terms for their role as second delivery partner on Phase 2 of the Meridian cloud migration
PURPOSE: Counter-propose on day rate for specialist Azure engineers — negotiate from their opening position of £1,150/day to an acceptable position of no more than £1,012/day (12% reduction)
CONTEXT: Meridian needs CloudBridge urgently — the decision to engage them was made by the steering group on 2 June; the contract needs to be signed by 15 June to protect the Phase 2 start date in July. CloudBridge knows this. Their opening day rate of £1,150 is above market; benchmarking shows comparable Azure/Entra engineers at £950–£1,050/day from accredited UK partners. Meridian's approved budget for Phase 2 external delivery is £180k. At £1,150/day for two engineers over 4 months (approximately 80 working days each), the cost would be £184k — £4k over the approved envelope.
INPUT:
  Meridian's position:
  - Target rate: £1,012/day per engineer (within the £180k budget envelope)
  - Acceptable range: £1,012–£1,050/day (at £1,050 with some scope flexibility, the engagement stays within budget)
  - Non-negotiable: the engagement must begin by 14 July; contract must be signed by 15 June
  - Flexibility: willing to commit to a longer engagement (6 months rather than 4) if the rate is at £1,012; willing to provide a reference and a case study for CloudBridge's portfolio on completion

  CloudBridge's known position:
  - Opened at £1,150/day
  - Their engineers are in demand; they have other potential placements
  - They want this engagement for the profile (Meridian is a regulated financial services firm — good for their credentials)
DESIRED_OUTCOME: CloudBridge agrees in principle to a rate at or below £1,050/day by 10 June, enabling contract finalisation by 15 June.
TONE: Professional, direct, collaborative — not desperate despite the time pressure
LENGTH: 250–350 words
CONSTRAINTS: Do not reveal that the budget ceiling is exactly £180k. Do not reference the timeline pressure explicitly — it weakens the position. Offer the 6-month extension as a value trade.
```

## Example output

---

**Re: Phase 2 Delivery Engagement — Day Rate Proposal**

James,

Thank you for the detailed proposal. The CloudBridge team clearly has the right experience profile for Phase 2, and the steering group was pleased with how quickly you were able to mobilise. The goal on both sides is a successful, well-delivered programme — and we want to put terms in place that reflect that shared interest.

On day rate, we are proposing £1,012 per engineer per day. Our benchmarking of current rates for accredited Azure and Entra specialists in the UK mid-market positions this at a fair point — and frankly, the regulatory environment and programme visibility this engagement offers represent real value to CloudBridge's portfolio in this space.

I understand you may have competing demand for these engineers. To address that directly: we are in a position to offer a longer engagement commitment of six months rather than four, which would provide greater certainty for your resourcing plan. We would also be pleased to provide a formal reference and support a case study on completion — both of which carry weight when tendering for regulated financial services clients.

We recognise this is a meaningful project and we want the commercial terms to reflect a genuine partnership. The rate we have proposed does that. A rate at or above £1,100 puts pressure on scope that we would rather avoid.

Could you confirm whether the proposed terms work by 10 June? That will allow us to finalise the contract and get both teams aligned on the Phase 2 mobilisation plan.

Best regards,
[Name]
Meridian Advisory

## Review checklist

- [ ] Opening references the shared interest or common goal — not the sender's demands
- [ ] The position is stated precisely — specific numbers or terms, not vague requests
- [ ] A brief rationale is given — not an over-explanation
- [ ] The counterparty's position is acknowledged genuinely
- [ ] A specific trade-off or path forward is proposed — not just a demand
- [ ] No ultimatums unless the sender is prepared to act on them
- [ ] Flexibility areas are implied without fully revealing the range
- [ ] Genuine constraints are communicated without closing the conversation
- [ ] Assumptions about the counterparty are framed as such
- [ ] British English used throughout
- [ ] Tone is professional and collaborative — not aggressive or appeasing
